# Prediction Markets as a News Signal: Accuracy, Extreme Confidence, and Criticism

Scope note: researched 2026-11-04. Several sources were reached only through search-result snippets or secondary summaries. These are flagged. Claims about Nov 3–4, 2026 are marked **[2026: verified]** when a mainstream outlet confirms them, and **[2026: UNVERIFIED]** otherwise.

## (a) How accurate have election/current-affairs prediction markets been?

### Takeaway
On average, election markets are roughly as good as or somewhat better than raw polls, especially far from the election. They are not reliably better than a careful reading of polls or a good model. Recent large-scale studies of Kalshi and Polymarket find good but imperfect calibration that varies by domain and by time to expiry. Political markets in particular tend to be *underconfident*: prices are compressed toward 50%. Famous misses (Brexit, Trump 2016, the 2022 "red wave") show that markets can share the polls' errors, while 2024 is the headline "win."

### Cited Findings

**Classic academic literature**
- Berg, Nelson & Rietz (2008, *International Journal of Forecasting* 24(2):285–300) compared the Iowa Electronic Markets (IEM) with 964 polls over five presidential elections. The market was closer to the outcome 74% of the time, and it beat the polls in every election when forecasting more than 100 days out — [IEM summary](https://iemweb.biz.uiowa.edu/about-iem/research/prediction-market-accuracy-in-the-long-run/); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:intfor:v:24:y:2008:i:2:p:285-300)
- A commentator noted that these results are about *relative* accuracy against single polls, not absolute accuracy — [Cornell INFO2040 blog](https://blogs.cornell.edu/info2040/2015/11/17/how-accurate-are-prediction-markets)
- Erikson & Wlezien (2008, *Public Opinion Quarterly*): "By our tests, the IEM election markets are not better than trial-heat polls for predicting elections... by a reasonable as opposed to naive reading of the polls, the polls dominate the markets" — [paper PDF](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Erikson-and-Wlezien-POQ-2008.pdf)
- Erikson & Wlezien (2012, *Electoral Studies*): market prices are far better predictors when polls are absent, but "When we have both market prices and polls, prices add nothing to election prediction beyond polls" — [paper PDF](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Erikson-and-Wlezien-Electoral-Studies-2012-1.pdf)
- Rothschild (2009, *POQ*) reportedly found that prices *debiased* for the favorite-longshot effect beat polling models, which in turn beat raw markets. This is secondhand only — [Substack summary](https://simonm.substack.com/p/does-academia-think-markets-are-less)

**Modern Kalshi/Polymarket calibration studies**
- Le (2026, arXiv:2602.19520, preprint) covers 64.7M Kalshi trades on 210,608 contracts and 227.6M Polymarket trades on about 116k contracts. Four components explain 87.3% of calibration variance. Miscalibration grows with time to expiry: the logistic slope is 0.99 under 1 hour and 1.32 beyond 1 month (a slope above 1 means underconfident). Politics shows *persistent underconfidence* (slopes 0.93–1.83), with the same direction on Polymarket (mean slope 1.31). In the paper's worked example, a 70¢ political contract one week out implies about 83% true probability. Prices outside 5–95¢ were excluded, so the paper does not address 98%+ prices — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v1)
- Bürgi et al. (2025, via a CEPR/VoxEU summary) analyzed over 300k Kalshi contracts and found a favorite-longshot bias: low-priced contracts win less often than their prices imply. Takers lose about 32% on average and makers about 10%. Accuracy improves near expiry — [pulse.bot summary of the VoxEU column](https://www.pulse.bot/fintech/news/the-economics-of-the-kalshi-prediction-market-e2d9cb54-3f70-44f5-af9c-259213af94bb). Secondary source; primary not fetched.
- Polymarket's self-reported accuracy page shows 98.6% "accuracy" 4 hours before resolution, 96.6% at 1 day, 94.2% at 1 week and 90.3–90.4% at 1 month, with a Brier score of 0.0599. Caveats: it is self-published; "accuracy" only means the leading outcome won, so 51% and 99% count the same; 78.3% of markets resolved "No," which flatters naive accuracy; there is no baseline; and the market count is not given — [Polymarket accuracy](https://polymarket.com/accuracy)

### Inferences
- In 2026 the favorites won both chambers, so this is a "hit" for markets in the binary sense. One hit at 75–94% pre-election odds is weak evidence of calibration either way.
- The Le (2026) finding that political markets are *underconfident* cuts against the intuition that a 98% price is overconfident. Over the 5–95¢ range, favorites in political markets won *more* often than prices implied. That study does not cover prices above 95¢, though.

## (b) What does 98%+ confidence before polls close mean?

### Takeaway
A 98%+ price on election night usually reflects *information already arriving*: eastern-state returns, needle-style models, and early and absentee counts. Polls close in waves, so "before polls close" often means after the first states have reported. Mechanically, prices near 99¢ carry little information about tail risk: the upside is tiny, capital costs and fees bind, and the favorite-longshot bias and thin books distort the extremes. Treat such a price as "traders think it's over." It is not an independent, calibrated 98% estimate, and markets have priced Remain at 90% and Clinton at about 83% on the eve of losses.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 timeline: the Senate was at 73–77% on the morning of Nov 3 — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/2026-midterms-prediction-market-odds-220150918.html). Kalshi had the House at 100% by about 9 pm ET, "a couple hours into" results — [Mediaite](https://www.mediaite.com/media/news/prediction-markets-say-dems-now-favored-to-win-the-house-by-an-absolutely-jaw-dropping-90seats/). So the jump to extreme confidence happened *during* the count, driven by early returns, not before any votes were known.
- Kalshi payout rules say contracts settle only after designated media calls. Many races will be "too close to call" on election night, which delays resolution — [Covers](https://www.covers.com/politics/midterm-election-prediction-markets-payout-rules-2026). Implication: a 98–99¢ price before a call partly reflects settlement timing and the cost of locking up capital, not only uncertainty.
- Favorite-longshot bias: low-priced (longshot) contracts win less often than implied — [Kalshi study summary](https://www.pulse.bot/fintech/news/the-economics-of-the-kalshi-prediction-market-e2d9cb54-3f70-44f5-af9c-259213af94bb). Rothschild says this bias matters near 0 and 1 and should be corrected before reading prices as probabilities — [summary](https://simonm.substack.com/p/does-academia-think-markets-are-less)
- Calibration becomes nearly perfect inside the last hour (slope 0.99 under 1h) — [arXiv 2602.19520](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v1). Polymarket reports 98.6% accuracy 4h before resolution, but the sample includes many near-certain markets — [Polymarket accuracy](https://polymarket.com/accuracy)
- Trade-size effect: on Kalshi political markets, large trades (over 100 contracts) are *more* underconfident (slope 1.74 vs 1.19), which the author suggests reflects institutional or arbitrage behavior. The effect was not replicated on Polymarket — [arXiv 2602.19520](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v1)
- *Whales and private information:* In 2024, the French trader "Théo" made Trump bets across several accounts. Estimates run from about $48M (4 accounts) to about $85M (11 accounts) — [Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/business-news/how-trump-whale-theo-made-48-million-neighbor-effect/482539). Chainalysis estimated net profit at $78.7M — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/324996/french-polymarket-whale-us-election-profit-france-ban). He commissioned his own "neighbor" polls, and the WSJ said it could not verify that he had no political agenda — [Tildes quoting WSJ](https://tildes.net/~misc/1jx8/how_the_trump_whale_correctly_called_the_election). A single whale can move a price, which can carry real information or can be noise or manipulation.
- *Wash trading:* A Columbia SSRN paper (not peer reviewed) classified about 25% of Polymarket's historical volume as likely wash trading. The weekly share peaked near 60% in Dec 2024. By category: sports 45%, election markets 17%, politics 12% — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/07/polymarket-wash-trading-inflated-prediction-markets-columbia-research); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347842/columbia-study-25-polymarket-volume-wash-trading?amp=1). Headline volume figures (such as "$1.4B on midterms") therefore overstate genuine depth.
- *Manipulation susceptibility:* FAIR's Jim Naureckas says markets are "highly susceptible to gaming." Critics add that volumes are far smaller than stock markets', so wealthy actors can move odds — [search summary of FAIR / New Yorker coverage](https://fair.org/home/you-have-a-double-standard-about-which-populations-are-considered-roulette-chips/)
- *Insider information:* A new Polymarket account turned about $30k into more than $400k on a Maduro-removal contract hours before the U.S. operation. This led Rep. Ritchie Torres to introduce H.R. 7004 (Public Integrity in Financial Prediction Markets Act of 2026) — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/384200/rep-torres-targets-prediction-market-insider-trading-after-trader-nets-400k-on-maduros-capture-report); [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/01/05/congressman-torres-prediction-markets/). A former CFTC attorney said the agency's resources make policing hard — [Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2026/01/12/polymarket-kalshi-insider-trading-prediction-markets-cftc-torres-titus-venezuela)
- *Garbage in, garbage out:* traders rely heavily on polls, so if the polls are wrong, the markets will be too — [Vanderbilt/arXiv 2507.08921 context](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08921)
- *Historical extreme-confidence misses:* Remain was at about 90% on polling day — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/personal-finance/financial-predictions-lose-their-edge-after-brexit-and-trump-1.2718171). Clinton was at about 82–83% the day before — [Washington Times](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2016/nov/13/donald-trump-earns-early-bettors-big-payoff-sticks/)

### Inferences
- For the user's dashboard: the "98%+ D House and D Senate before polls close" reading was most likely set after East Coast polls closed and the first returns came in, as a market *summary of early returns*, not foresight. The outcome matched. Displaying it as "traders' view, not a call" is the defensible framing.
- At 98¢, "2% chance it's wrong" is an upper bound polluted by fees, capital lock-up until settlement, and longshot buyers. Historically, the bigger risk is shared error with polls (2016, 2022), not random 2% noise.
- It is a cheap, fast aggregator of public information, so it is useful as a "where is the conventional wisdom" indicator. It is not an independent source of facts.

## (c) Critiques of treating market odds as news

### Takeaway
Critics in journalism, policy and law raise four concerns: markets are not representative and not auditable; newsroom partnerships create undisclosed conflicts (CNN and CNBC with Kalshi, Dow Jones with Polymarket); markets invite insider trading and manipulation; and their legal status as gambling or as derivatives is contested in more than 20 lawsuits. Defenders treat odds as one real-time input among polls and models.

### Cited Findings
- **CJR, "A Bet Is Not a Poll"** (C.J. Robinson, Tow Center, May 7, 2026): markets give one probability from bettors, not a representative sample. Their moves can't be audited, and they risk projecting false certainty. Sports were almost 90% of Kalshi volume in 2025 (Paradigm data). G. Elliott Morris calls uncritical citation "incredibly irresponsible" and says "Absolutely no one has to be reporting this information." Nathaniel Rakich (Votebeat) says a market is "not a representative sample, the way a poll is" and that people "round" 70% "up to a hundred" — [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/a-bet-is-not-a-poll-polymarket-kalshi-journalists-wary-predict-future.php)
- CJR also interviewed Dustin Gouker (Event Horizon), who takes a mixed view: markets shouldn't be "a source of truth, or the only input" — [CJR interview](https://www.cjr.org/the-interview/are-prediction-markets-actually-good-for-journalism-kalshi-polymarket-dow-jones-cnn.php)
- **Media partnerships:** Kalshi became CNN's official partner in Dec 2025, with odds featured on Harry Enten's show and elsewhere — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2025-12-02-kalshi-cnn-partner-381164). Public Notice reported that CNBC hosts did not disclose the Kalshi relationship on air, that CNN aired "The Odds" segment at least 115 times, and that financial terms are undisclosed — [Public Notice](https://publicnotice.co/p/kalshi-cnn-cnbc). A reported exclusivity clause means CNN doesn't cite Polymarket — per the search summary of the same coverage. Oliver Darcy, in The New Yorker, called it "a very legitimizing force for a gambling company" — [New Yorker (via scour.ing mirror)](https://scour.ing/@matmat/p/https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-lede/americas-betting-craze-has-spread-to-its-news-networks). CNN says markets are "just one source of data" complementing polling — per the same search summary.
- **The Intercept** (Apr 28, 2026): many contracts are built on news reports, so reporters supply raw material for bets, and odds crowd out coverage of substance — [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2026/04/28/kalshi-polymarket-news-journalism-partnerships/)
- **Newsroom ethics policy:** Nieman Lab reports the New York Times told staff that betting on news events via prediction markets "is a violation of our principles" — [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/the-new-york-times-tells-staffers-that-betting-on-news-events-with-prediction-markets-is-a-violation-of-our-principles/)

- **Regulatory and legal:** On Apr 6, 2026, the Third Circuit ruled 2-1 that federal commodities law preempts New Jersey's attempt to block Kalshi sports contracts — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/kalshi-wins-preliminary-ruling-new-jersey-sports-contracts/). - **Probability literacy:** CJR cites research showing people struggle to interpret election probabilities, and a 2022 Tow Center report recommending an emphasis on uncertainty — [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/a-bet-is-not-a-poll-polymarket-kalshi-journalists-wary-predict-future.php)

### Inferences
- For a personal dashboard, the main journalistic critiques (undisclosed sponsorship, false certainty) apply as design guidance. Label odds as "market price (traders' bets), not results or calls." Show the source and a timestamp. Never let an odds move trigger "call" language. The project already restricts calls to the AP and network data feed, which matches this.
- Kalshi's partnership with CNN means CNN's on-air odds are a sponsored data feed. That is relevant when comparing "network says" with "markets say."
