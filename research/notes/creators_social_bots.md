# Creator / Indie Layer of Prediction-Market Journalism (newsletters, social accounts, bots, podcasts, OSS)

Research date: 2026-11-04. About 17 tool calls. Most hard evidence comes from investigative coverage (CJR Tow Center, Politico, NPR, WSJ via TechSpot, Popular Information). Audience figures are sparse.

## Newsletters / Substacks: who publishes "what the markets say"?

### Takeaway
The newsletter layer is dominated by market-owned or market-affiliated outlets. These include Polymarket's own "The Oracle", Nate Silver (a paid Polymarket advisor), and Eventual (a Polymarket data partner). Substack's platform-level Polymarket partnership pushes live odds embeds into many independent newsletters. Truly independent, weekly "markets this week" newsletters exist, but they are small and mostly launched in 2025–2026. Some (Event Horizon, Popular Information) are partly critical of the markets.

### Cited Findings
- **The Oracle by Polymarket** (Polymarket's own Substack) launched in July 2024 alongside Substack's Polymarket embeds. Substack described it as "insights and analysis gleaned from the thousands of markets" active on Polymarket, with a regular roundup of notable markets plus deeper analysis of current issues. — [Substack announcement](https://on.substack.com/p/dynamic-prediction-market-tables); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/242455/polymarket-embeds-on-substack); [Nieman Lab, July 2024](https://niemanlab.org/2024/07/im-betting-youre-gonna-see-more-news-betting-in-your-news-reading)
  - Reletter lists Neil Paine and Terry Lee as contributors (unverified). One aggregator claims more than 70,000 subscribers (unverified, third-party). — [Reletter](https://reletter.com/publications/polymarkets-substack); [newsletterly](https://newsletterly.io/newsletter/the-oracle-by-polymarket-substack)
- **Substack–Polymarket expanded partnership (Feb 20, 2026).**
  - What it adds: "native tools" for sharing prediction-market data, auto-refreshing embeds, and charts allowed in Notes as well as posts.
  - Adoption: per CEO Chris Best, one in five of the 250 highest-paid Substacks had already adopted the features.
  - Polymarket's framing: journalism is better when "backed by live markets." — [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/substack-polymarket-expanded-partnership)
  - Polymarket is reportedly also sponsoring a cohort of Substack creators who adopt the tools. — [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/substack-isnt-kidding-about-pushing-prediction-market-bets-into-its-newsletters/); [NewsNation](https://www.newsnationnow.com/business/tech/substack-polymarket-partnership/amp/) (the sponsorship detail comes from search snippets; terms and amounts were not found)
- **Nate Silver / Silver Bulletin.**
  - Silver joined Polymarket as a paid advisor in July 2024. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/305703/polymarket-taps-election-statistician-nate-silver-as-advisor); [CoinDesk](https://coindesk.com/business/2024/07/17/polymarket-hires-nate-silver-after-taking-in-265m-of-bets-on-us-election-report)
  - A Silver Bulletin post (SBSQ #26, around Nov 2025) asks "do prediction markets make..." and still describes him as a Polymarket advisor. — [Silver Bulletin](https://www.natesilver.net/p/sbsq-26-do-prediction-markets-make)
  - I found no 2026 confirmation of whether the role continues.
- **Eventual** is a San Francisco-based outlet "staffed by working prediction market traders." It publishes daily written pieces on Substack, running since early July 2026, plus a show (twice weekly per one description, weekly per another). It has a **data partnership with Polymarket**. — [Sovereign Magazine, Jul 29, 2026](https://www.sovereignmagazine.com/article/prediction-market-traders-newsroom-eventual)
- **Event Horizon** (nexteventhorizon.substack.com) is a weekly analysis and roundup of prediction-market news. It is also critical: it published "Polymarket, A 'Source Of Truth,' Is Spreading Misinformation On Social Media." — [About page](https://nexteventhorizon.substack.com/about); [post](https://nexteventhorizon.substack.com/p/is-polymarket-a-source-of-truth)
- **Prediction Market Pulse** (predictionnews.substack.com) is a weekly newsletter "for prediction market enthusiasts." — [Substack](https://predictionnews.substack.com)
- **"It's Not Gambling"** is a prediction-market newsletter listed on Reletter (details not verified). — [Reletter](https://reletter.com/publications/its-not-gambling)
- **The Market Oracle** (DEV Community / newsletter) is a weekly "prediction market intelligence" newsletter whose sample portfolio is explicitly fictional. — [DEV issue](https://dev.to/marketoracle/kalshi-at-22b-polymarket-raises-400m-and-our-portfolio-is-up-336-863)
- **Substack's own "What the markets are saying"** post on its on.substack.com blog promotes the format. — [Substack](https://on.substack.com/p/what-the-markets-are-saying?open=false)
- **Forecasting Newsletter (Nuño Sempere)** is monthly and has over 2,000 subscribers per its own description.
  - 2025 issues include #2/2025 (Feb 9) and #3/2025. #9/2025 covers opportunity markets, AI forecasters and Polymarket's builders program.
  - I found no 2026 issues. Sempere is head of foresight at Sentinel. — [forecasting.substack.com](https://forecasting.substack.com); [LessWrong #3/2025](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/RJ8sX55eStb57Cjy9/forecasting-newsletter-3-2025-long-march-through-the); [Reletter](https://reletter.com/publications/forecasting)
- **Scott Alexander, "Mantic Monday" (Astral Codex Ten).** The latest found installment is "The Monkey's Paw Curls" (Jan 2026; feeds disagree between Jan 13 and Jan 30).
  - He says markets "have gone to the moon," from millions to billions in monthly volume, and writes "insofar as my advocacy helped make this possible, I am bad."
  - The post discusses regulators, Polymarket moving onshore and resolution disputes.
  - It is narrative and essayistic, and screenshots specific market odds. — [Audioscrape transcript](https://www.audioscrape.com/podcast/astral-codex-ten-podcast/episode/mantic-monday-the-monkeys-paw-curls-48420); [Audible](https://www.audible.com/podcast/Mantic-Monday-The-Monkeys-Paw-Curls/B0GKM7QQ17)
  - He ran the ACX 2025 Prediction Contest with Metaculus ($10k prize pool). — [Metaculus](https://metaculus.com/tournament/ACX2025/?page=3)
- **Maxim Lott, ElectionBettingOdds.com** is an aggregator that converts betting-market prices into candidate odds; its software checks several sites every minute. Lott is a senior producer of "Stossel on Reason" and writes the "Maximum Truth" Substack. — [Reason profile](https://reason.com/people/maxim-lott/); [Substack](https://substack.com/@maximumtruth); [Fox News](https://foxnews.com/politics/betting-odds-have-flipped-to-favor-trump-expert-says.amp); [Based Camp interview](https://basedcamppodcast.substack.com/p/election-betting-why-gamblers-keep)
  - I did not find any 2026-midterm-specific coverage from him.
- **Popular Information (Judd Legum)** is a critical newsletter that investigated Polymarket's official account and paid affiliates. It reported that Polymarket ended sponsorship of some election-misinformation posts after its investigation. — [Popular Information](https://popular.info/p/the-truth-machine-is-lying-to-you); [update](https://popular.info/p/update-polymarket-ends-sponsorship)

### Inferences
- The most-read "what the markets say" newsletter voices are mostly financially tied to Polymarket: its own newsletter, its advisor and its data partners. Substack's platform-wide integration means odds now appear inside many independent political newsletters without those writers being affiliates.
- The independent weekly-roundup niche (Event Horizon, Prediction Market Pulse, Eventual) appears new in 2025–2026. The rationalist/forecasting community (Sempere, Alexander) is the longest-running independent voice, and it is now partly ambivalent or critical.

### Gaps
- No verified subscriber counts for The Oracle, Silver Bulletin, Eventual, Event Horizon or Prediction Market Pulse.
- Richard Hanania and Dan Pfeiffer: I found no sourced evidence either one regularly publishes market-odds coverage (not searched in depth).
- Terms of Polymarket's Substack creator-cohort sponsorship are unknown.
- Whether Nate Silver's Polymarket advisory role continued through 2026 is unconfirmed.

## Social accounts and bots: official accounts, "breaking news" posts, affiliate news accounts, whale trackers

### Takeaway
Polymarket's official X account, and a network of badge-carrying affiliate "news" accounts, post "BREAKING"-style headlines paired with odds. Several are documented as false or misleading. Collectively, affiliates out-reach the official account by more than 3x in impressions. Kalshi runs a similar partner network and has repeatedly purged affiliates. Whale and market-move trackers are mostly Telegram bots and small indie tools. I found no notable Bluesky odds bots.

### Cited Findings
- **CJR Tow Center (C.J. Robinson, Mar 25, 2026).** The Tow Center found at least 11 X accounts with "news" or "updates" in their bios listed as Polymarket affiliates or referencing work with the company. — [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/polymarket-affiliates-are-spreading-misinformation-on-x.php); [Nieman Lab summary](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/polymarket-affiliates-are-spreading-misinformation-on-x/)
  - Named Polymarket affiliates: @NewsWire_US, @unusual_whales, @LeadingReport, @RedLineNewsUSA, @Breaking911, @PopBase, @sentdefender, @zerohedge and @PolymarketIntel (formerly @UAWeapons).
  - Individuals with "paid partnership" posts: Piers Morgan, Laura Loomer, Savanah Hernandez and Harry Sisson.
  - Kalshi partner accounts: @BNONews and @WhaleInsider.
  - @NewsWire_US averages over 200M impressions per month (Junkipedia data). Affiliates collectively draw more than 3x the official Polymarket account's impressions.
  - Example false posts:
    - A @NewsWire_US NASA-elimination post got over 20M impressions.
    - An @unusual_whales post said "70% of Realtors didn't sell a home" when the real figure is 5%. It got over 5M impressions.
    - @LeadingReport was rebuilt into a Polymarket-linked feed, and Pangram flagged several of its posts as fully AI-generated.
  - Most affiliates carry a Polymarket or PolymarketMoney badge, which requires an invitation. Compensation was unclear: direct pay, affiliate links, or none.
  - Kalshi dropped its official badges in February after an X policy change.
- **New York Times review** found "hundreds of false and misleading posts" on Polymarket's social feeds (as summarized by Wikipedia). — [Wikipedia: Polymarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymarket)
- **Official Polymarket account incidents.**
  - Jan 2026: a "breaking" post attributing advice to Jeff Bezos, which Bezos publicly denied. — [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/jeff-bezos-denies-polymarket-fake-news); [KuCoin flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/polymarket-faces-backlash-for-alleged-fake-news-jeff-bezos-denies-claim)
  - A false "babies receive 72 injections" graphic, and a false NYC grocery-ID claim. — [Popular Information](https://popular.info/p/the-truth-machine-is-lying-to-you)
  - A Zelensky "apology" post controversy. — [Daily Dot](https://dailydot.com/polymarket-zelensky-apology-post-russia)
- **Popular Information** found a network of at least 16 influencers pushing election misinformation in Polymarket-sponsored posts on X. Polymarket responded that its guidelines "explicitly prohibit affiliates from providing misleading or false information," and some posts were deleted. — [Popular Information](https://popular.info/p/the-truth-machine-is-lying-to-you); [update](https://popular.info/p/update-polymarket-ends-sponsorship)
- **Kalshi affiliate purges.**
  - Kalshi cut the affiliate account InsiderWire (Aug 2025).
  - It removed badges from at least three affiliates after antisemitic posts (Dec 2025).
  - X's Nikita Bier warned in Feb–Mar 2026 that undisclosed prediction-market promoters face suspension. — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/kalshi-purges-twitter-social-media-affiliates-antisemitic-posts/); [Catenaa](https://catenaa.com/markets/cryptocurrencies/kalshi-removes-x-affiliate-badges-after-rule-change); [CryptoRank](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/294c3-x-after-prediction-markets-kalshi-badges)
- **Polymarket vs Kalshi rivalry** has escalated into attack ads and "death markets" fights. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/financial-accounting/death-markets-and-attack-ads-prediction-rivalry-turns-nasty)
- **Whale and alert bots** are mostly Telegram, often open source:
  - PolyBit, a Bitquery tutorial bot that alerts by trade size, price, wallet or market. — [Bitquery docs](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/usecases/polymarket-tg-alerts-bot/)
  - Polymonit (Qoreon Labs), with Telegram and email alerts. — [QuickNode](https://www.quicknode.com/fr/builders-guide/tools/polymonit-by-qoreon-labs-ltd)
  - polytracker, a GitHub repo flagging whale trades over $10k, fresh wallets and coordinated buying, with Discord and Telegram alerts. — [mirror of himyeticapital/polytracker](https://gh.r-mj.com/himyeticapital/polytracker)
  - PolyInsight, a Telegram bot. — [mirror of Wammero/PolyInsight](https://gh.hhj520.top/Wammero/PolyInsight)
  - al1enjesus/polymarket-whales. — [GitHub mirror](https://github.blesdmm.dns-dynamic.net/al1enjesus/polymarket-whales)
  - WhaleTrack, an indie hacker's site with an X bot and a Telegram channel that gets alerts about 10 minutes earlier; a paywall is planned. — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-built-a-real-time-polymarket-whale-tracker-heres-where-i-m-at-after-launch-d70d64059a)
  - The pm.wiki directory lists polywhaler, polylerts, whale-tracker-livid (real-time alerts $29/mo, free tier delayed 1h) and others. — [pm.wiki](https://pm.wiki/projects/polymarket-whale-tracker)
- **Unusual Whales** (@unusual_whales), a large finance account, is listed by CJR as claiming a Polymarket partnership without a badge. — [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/polymarket-affiliates-are-spreading-misinformation-on-x.php)

### Inferences
- The "prediction market newswire" on X is largely a monetized affiliate layer. Headline-plus-odds posts from accounts styled as news wires are the dominant format. The format is numbers-first (a percentage plus a headline) and weak on sourcing.
- Whale tracking is the main bot genre. Telegram is the main channel, followed by Discord, with X a distant third.

### Gaps
- Follower counts for @Polymarket, @Kalshi and most affiliate accounts were not found in the sources.
- I found no evidence of notable Bluesky odds bots, and Bluesky-specific searches returned nothing relevant.
- No Discord/Telegram community sizes were found.

## Influencer / creator programs and FTC/CFTC disclosure concerns

### Takeaway
Both Polymarket and Kalshi pay creators per post or per month, reportedly up to $500 per post or $2–3k per month, often without disclosure. The documented abuses are:
- Politico's June 2026 exposé of payments from the Polymarket CMO's personal PayPal.
- WSJ reporting on staged "win" videos.
- Paid election-fraud posts during the LA mayoral primary.

These prompted affiliate rule changes, but no FTC or CFTC enforcement was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Politico (Jun 5, 2026).** Polymarket CMO Matthew Modabber sent more than $2.5M to over 800 people from a personal PayPal account between Jan 2025 and Feb 2026. — [Bitcoin.com summary](https://news.bitcoin.com/polymarket-cmo-used-personal-paypal-to-pay-influencers-politico-reports/); [Ground News](https://ground.news/article/polymarket-influencer-payments-draw-scrutiny-over-undisclosed-x-promotions_a174d6); [RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/polymarket-influencer-payments-disclosure-shayne-coplan)
  - At least $350k went to roughly two dozen influencers, and around 20 of them made about 490 posts without paid-partnership disclosure.
  - After the xAI partnership announcement, paid influencers posted supportive content within hours of each other.
  - Polymarket called creator partnerships standard business practice.
  - Secondary summaries differ on "20" vs "two dozen". I could not reach the Politico original.
- **WSJ (June 2026, via TechSpot and others).** Polymarket paid at least 10 influencers to post videos pretending to win large sums on fake wagers on look-alike sites. Creators were paid around $2,000–3,000 per month and told not to disclose. — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/112856-polymarket-paid-influencers-fake-winning-bets-built-dummy.html); [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/polymarket-viral-wins-staged-fake-platforms-1804276); [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/polymarket-reportedly-paid-creators-to-post-fake-betting-videos-showing-fabricated-wins/)
- **NPR (Jun 7, 2026), LA mayoral primary.** — [NPR via WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2026-06-07/kalshi-and-polymarket-crack-down-on-paid-influencers-claiming-election-fraud); [NEPM](https://www.nepm.org/2026-06-07/on-x-influencers-are-using-prediction-markets-to-sow-distrust-about-election-results); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/415637/)
  - Pay rates: both firms offered creators up to $500 per post (two former partnership staffers, anonymous).
  - Posts named:
    - Mila Joy (about 500k followers) reshared Polymarket odds with fraud claims; her "paid partnership" tag remained.
    - Kalshi asked David Freeman ("Gunther Eagleman") to delete a "CA cheating" post.
    - Polymarket removed paid tags from Benny Johnson's and Kangmin Lee's posts.
    - Matt Gaetz was reportedly a paid Polymarket contributor.
  - Kalshi now bars affiliates from questioning election integrity. Polymarket has no specific election clause but says such posts violate its false-information rules.
  - Disclosure law: the article says FTC rules adopted in 2024 make undisclosed sponsored posts illegal, but there have been no enforcement announcements.
  - Semafor first reported the crackdown.
- **Platform rules.** X has added a paid-promotion label (users self-report), but X does not show which company a user partnered with. — [CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/polymarket-affiliates-are-spreading-misinformation-on-x.php)
- **Polymarket's esports quote-retweet program** has written disclosure guidelines, a contrast to the X political posts. — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/polymarket-esports-paid-twitter.md)
- **Conflicts of interest.** Donald Trump Jr. has invested in Polymarket and advises Kalshi. — [AV Club citing PBS](https://www.avclub.com/substack-polymarket-expanded-partnership); [AffPapa on Kalshi share gift](https://affpapa.com/kalshi-criticized-for-gifting-donald-trump-jr-300k-in-shares/)

### Inferences
- A significant share of "creator coverage" of market odds on X in 2025–2026 was paid. Undisclosed payment was common enough that any odds-as-news post from a large account should be treated as potentially sponsored.
- Regulatory risk sits with the FTC (endorsement disclosure) rather than the CFTC. I found no CFTC action on marketing.

### Gaps
- I found no FTC or CFTC enforcement or statement specific to prediction-market influencers.
- The Politico original was not fetched directly; figures come from secondary summaries.
- No full list of paid creators was found.

## Podcasts / YouTube

### Takeaway
Several 2025–2026 prediction-market podcasts exist. Many of the bigger ones are co-produced or "powered by" Polymarket. Indie shows are small, and no audience numbers were found.

### Cited Findings
- **"What Are the Odds?"** is Polymarket's weekly show with Dear Media, launched June 2026. It blends celebrity and internet news with live odds, with rotating Dear Media hosts (e.g., Claudia and Jackie Oshry of "The Toast"). — [The ATDB, Jun 18, 2026](https://www.theatdb.com/news/polymarket-and-dear-media-team-for-weekly-podcast-what-are-the-odds-exclusive)
- **True Odds: A Prediction Markets Podcast** is a weekly Stocktwits series "powered by Polymarket." — [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/id1860670845)
- **Indie and other shows:**
  - Inside Prediction Markets (hosts Chris and Henry; YouTube). — [Rephonic](https://rephonic.com/podcasts/inside-prediction-markets)
  - Poly Marks, a comedy show from brothers Andrew and Matt with moderator Joel. — [Buzzsprout](https://polymarks.buzzsprout.com/)
  - The PredictIt Podcast (@PredictItPod; weekly; YouTube @predictitofficial). PredictIt's own show.
  - Odds On Open, which interviewed the top trader "Domer." — [Pocket Casts](https://pocketcasts.com/podcast/odds-on-open/a7cd8fc0-429e-013e-1c37-0acc26574db2/how-the-worlds-1-prediction-markets-trader-finds-edge-domer-on-trading-global-political-events/c6ee34dd-c368-4a47-81d2-70e7f982a229)

### Inferences
- The market companies are buying their way into entertainment and lifestyle podcasting (Dear Media) and finance media (Stocktwits) rather than relying on organic creators.

### Gaps
- No download or subscriber numbers for any show.
- No dedicated YouTube-native "odds of the week" channel with a verified audience was found.

## Open-source projects / dashboards (including TV-style displays)

### Takeaway
Plenty of open-source tooling exists for pulling odds and tracking whales. Nearly all of it is trader-oriented: bots, scanners and arbitrage tools. I found no open-source live "TV broadcast" style news display built on market APIs, which leaves room for a project like this one.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenTradex** (MIT) is a dashboard-first AI trading harness connecting to Polymarket, Kalshi and other venues. Its layout includes a news feed, positions and a market scanner. — [awesome.ecosyste.ms](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fdeonmenezes%2Fopentradex)
- **pauljump/polyfeeds** monitors the public data sources that resolve Polymarket and Kalshi markets, including election results and economic indicators. — [GitHub](https://github.com/pauljump/polyfeeds)
- **kuestcom/prediction-market** is an open-source prediction-market project. — [awesome.ecosyste.ms](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fkuestcom%2Fprediction-market)
- **Awesome Prediction Market Tools** is a curated list that includes PMXT, a unified cross-exchange API. — [mirror](https://gh.r-mj.com/aarora4/Awesome-Prediction-Market-Tools)
- **Streamlit World Cup prediction dashboard tutorial** covers Polymarket APIs, live odds, order book depth and history, refreshing on a timer. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/fatherson/building-a-real-time-world-cup-prediction-dashboard-with-polymarket-apis-python-and-streamlit-2b7b)
- **market-predict** is a live page comparing Kalshi, Polymarket and FRED, refreshed every 15 minutes and focused on SPY/QQQ. — [GitHub Pages](https://yichengyang-ethan.github.io/market-predict)
- **Paid scrapers and APIs:** an Apify scraper for Polymarket and Kalshi odds and arbitrage ([Apify](https://apify.com/scrapesage/prediction-markets-scraper)), and Odds-API.io's prediction-market feed ([Odds API blog](https://odds-api.io/blog/polymarket-kalshi-api-prediction-market-odds)).
- **Mainstream integration for context:** CNBC integrated Kalshi's data API into its news suite. — [search summary of DEV/other sources; see The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/prediction-markets-expand-from-defi-niche-to-global-news-sources)

### Inferences
- The OSS ecosystem mirrors the creator ecosystem: it is dominated by trading and whale alerts rather than journalism. A TV-style, narrative "markets newsroom" display appears to be an unoccupied open-source niche.

### Gaps
- GitHub was not searched directly through the API; repo popularity (stars) and maintenance status are unverified.
- Several repo links are third-party GitHub mirrors (gh.r-mj.com, gh.hhj520.top, github.blesdmm.dns-dynamic.net) rather than github.com originals.
