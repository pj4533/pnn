# Exchanges now own most "markets say" news

Yes, many people report on what prediction markets predict, but most of the reporting worth reading is paid for by the markets it covers. Polymarket runs its own newsletter (The Oracle), a podcast, a midterms hub and a network of affiliate "news" accounts on X. Kalshi runs its own newsroom (news.kalshi.com), an election-night "projection" feed, and paid data deals with CNN, CNBC, Fox and CBS. Dow Jones and Substack have exclusive Polymarket deals, and Google Finance shows both exchanges' odds. The independent layer is small and mostly made up of numbers-first aggregators (Election Betting Odds, PredictionMarkets.US, Predxy), trader tools and whale bots, a few new newsletters, and a critical forecasting-community fringe. On the signal itself: election markets are roughly as accurate as a sensible reading of the polls, not clearly better. They have shared the polls' big misses (Brexit, 2016, the 2022 "red wave"), and prices of 98¢ and up are shaped by fees, settlement delays and thin order books, not just belief. Your 98%+ reading at 8:44 PM ET was real and the outcome matched it, but it summarized early returns rather than predicting ahead of them: eve-of-election odds were about 93–94% for the House and about 75% for the Senate. The clearest gap I found is an independent, plain-English, live broadcast-style "markets newsroom". Nobody credible occupies it, partner networks deliberately keep odds off their election-night screens, and I found no open-source project like the one you built.

## Kalshi bought the TV networks, Polymarket bought print and platforms

The biggest audience for "what the markets say" in 2026 comes from paid distribution deals, not dedicated sites. **Kalshi became CNN's official prediction-market partner in December 2025.** Its data feeds a recurring segment called "The Odds", led by chief data analyst Harry Enten, with a "Kalshi-powered real-time news ticker" running during those segments ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/tvnewser/ticker-cnn-and-cnbc-strike-partnership-with-kalshi/)). Public Notice counted at least 115 airings of the segment ([Public Notice](https://publicnotice.co/p/kalshi-cnn-cnbc)). CNBC signed a multi-year exclusive the same week. Its prediction-market stories now carry the disclosure that "CNBC and Kalshi have a commercial relationship that includes customer acquisition and a minority investment," and Kalshi sponsored CNBC's "Trading the Midterms" conference in October ([Bloomberg via Tucson.com](https://tucson.com/news/nation-world/business/article_1500cb9f-37da-48fd-a340-e0f6706d498b.html)). Fox Corp followed in April 2026 with a paid "sponsored integration" that excludes political coverage ([TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/industry-news/deals-ma/kalshi-fox-corp-fox-news-deal)). CBS named Kalshi its midterm "prediction market sponsor" in October and placed contract prices beside its Battleground Tracker polling ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/cbs-news-partners-with-kalshi-for-midterm-election-data-oct-1-2026)). Data also flows the other way: since March 2026, **AP licenses its live vote counts and race calls to Kalshi**, which shows them side by side with its odds ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/02/kalshi-ap-elections-data)).

Polymarket took print, newsletters and social platforms. It is the exclusive prediction-market data provider for the WSJ, Barron's, MarketWatch and IBD ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260107511213/en)), and editors choose a "Featured Prediction Market" for the WSJ homepage ([Bloomberg via Tucson.com](https://tucson.com/news/nation-world/business/article_1500cb9f-37da-48fd-a340-e0f6706d498b.html)). Substack added live Polymarket embeds in 2024 and made the deal exclusive in February 2026 under the tagline "Journalism is better when it's backed by live markets". According to Substack's CEO, one in five of the 250 highest-earning Substacks had adopted the tools, and Polymarket reportedly sponsors a cohort of creators who use them ([AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/substack-polymarket-expanded-partnership); [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/02/polymarket-says-journalism-is-better-when-its-backed-by-live-markets-does-anyone-know-what-that-means/)). Polymarket is also X's "official prediction market partner" ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-06/musk-s-x-selects-polymarket-as-prediction-market-partner)). Google Finance and Search show odds from both exchanges ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/google-kalshi-polymarket-prediction-markets-rcna242670)). No financial terms for any of these deals have been disclosed.

The exchanges also publish their own news. **The Oracle by Polymarket** launched in July 2024 as the company's "first news product", offering roundups of notable markets plus analysis ([Substack](https://on.substack.com/p/dynamic-prediction-market-tables)). Polymarket added a weekly celebrity-and-news odds podcast with Dear Media in June 2026 ([The ATDB](https://www.theatdb.com/news/polymarket-and-dear-media-team-for-weekly-podcast-what-are-the-odds-exclusive)) and a midterms hub with more than 560 markets in September ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/polymarket-launches-2026-midterms-hub-302880619.html)). Kalshi runs a staffed newsroom with named writers ([Kalshi News](https://news.kalshi.com/authors)). On election night it ran a live page styled like a network decision desk, with "99% KALSHI PROJECTION" and "JUST IN" labels from 5:45 PM to 2:34 AM ET, plus a disclaimer that projections "do not imply market resolution or that races have been called" ([Kalshi News](https://news.kalshi.com/p/2026-midterm-election-night-live-projected-winners)). That page is the closest existing product to a live broadcast on market odds, and the house runs it.

Most of the aggregators (PredictionMarkets.US, Prediktion, Predxy, PredictMarketCap, Verso and others) are built for arbitrage and trading, not for readers ([Awesome-Prediction-Market-Tools](https://github.com/aarora4/Awesome-Prediction-Market-Tools)). The pivot at Adjacent News is telling. Its founder raised money only after turning the media product into financial infrastructure. That points to independent "market news" being hard to fund on its own.

The most visible layer is X, and much of it is paid. The **CJR Tow Center identified at least 11 "news"-styled X accounts tied to Polymarket**, including @NewsWire_US, which averages over 200M impressions a month. Together these affiliates out-reach the official Polymarket account by more than 3x, and several spread false headlines next to odds ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/polymarket-affiliates-are-spreading-misinformation-on-x.php)). Politico reported that Polymarket's CMO sent more than $2.5M to over 800 people from a personal PayPal account, many of whom posted without disclosure ([Bitcoin.com summary](https://news.bitcoin.com/polymarket-cmo-used-personal-paypal-to-pay-influencers-politico-reports/)). The WSJ found influencers paid $2,000–3,000 a month to stage fake winning bets ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/112856-polymarket-paid-influencers-fake-winning-bets-built-dummy.html)). NPR reported that both firms offered up to $500 per post, and that during the LA mayoral primary paid accounts used odds to spread election-fraud claims, which pushed Kalshi to bar affiliates from questioning election integrity ([NPR via WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2026-06-07/kalshi-and-polymarket-crack-down-on-paid-influencers-claiming-election-fraud)). Even Nate Silver, the best-known forecaster who cites market odds, has been a paid Polymarket advisor since 2024 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/305703/polymarket-taps-election-statistician-nate-silver-as-advisor)). The practical rule follows: **treat any odds-as-news post from a large account as potentially sponsored.**

## The signal is good, not oracle-good, and 99 cents is the least informative price

The academic record is mixed. The classic Iowa Electronic Markets study found the market closer to the outcome than individual polls 74% of the time ([Berg, Nelson & Rietz via IEM](https://iemweb.biz.uiowa.edu/about-iem/research/prediction-market-accuracy-in-the-long-run/)). Erikson and Wlezien, however, found that "when we have both market prices and polls, prices add nothing to election prediction beyond polls" ([Electoral Studies 2012](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Erikson-and-Wlezien-Electoral-Studies-2012-1.pdf)). A 2026 preprint covering more than 290M Kalshi and Polymarket trades finds political markets *underconfident*: favorites won more often than prices implied. In its example, a 70¢ contract one week out implied roughly 83%. Calibration was nearly perfect within the final hour. The study excluded prices above 95¢, so it says nothing about the extremes ([arXiv 2602.19520](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19520v1)). Polymarket's self-published "98.6% accuracy four hours out" counts a 51% favorite the same as a 99% one and includes many near-certain "No" markets ([Polymarket](https://polymarket.com/accuracy)).

The famous misses matter more than the averages. Remain was priced around 90% on Brexit polling day ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/personal-finance/financial-predictions-lose-their-edge-after-brexit-and-trump-1.2718171)). On Nov 7, 2022, PredictIt gave Democrats a 10% chance of holding the House, lower than Nate Silver's 16% ([InvestingLive](https://investinglive.com/news/what-prediction-markets-are-saying-about-republicans-chances-in-the-midterms-20221107/amp)). Traders read polls too, so when polls are wrong, markets tend to be wrong in the same direction ([arXiv 2507.08921](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08921)). The "markets beat the networks" story from 2024 came from Polymarket crossing 95% for Trump nearly six hours before AP's call ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2024-11-06-donald-trump-polymarket-324738)). That compares a probability with a decision desk's certainty standard, which is not a like-for-like comparison. The 2024 story also had a single French whale, "Théo", betting tens of millions on Trump ([Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/business-news/how-trump-whale-theo-made-48-million-neighbor-effect/482539)). A Columbia working paper classified about 25% of Polymarket's historical volume as likely wash trading ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/07/polymarket-wash-trading-inflated-prediction-markets-columbia-research)), so headline volume figures overstate real depth.

The 2026 midterms count as a clean binary hit. AP called the Senate for Democrats at 11:40 PM ET ([PBS/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/democrats-take-control-of-the-senate-after-winning-key-midterm-races)), and outlets report Democrats winning both chambers. Semafor credited markets with calling "the Democratic sweep of Congress by wider margins than most polling models", and noted misses on Texas governor and the Iowa and South Carolina Senate races ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/04/2026/prediction-markets-win-big-on-election-night)). One correct outcome at 75–94% pre-election odds says little about calibration either way.

## Your 98% was the count talking, which is still worth showing

The timing behind the observation is worth laying out. On the eve of the election, Kalshi had Democrats at **93% for the House and 75% for the Senate**, and Polymarket had 94.5% and 75.5% ([Variety](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/midterm-elections-odds-prediction-spending-house-and-senate-1236938339/)). Kalshi's Senate number actually slipped from 77% to 73% on the morning of Nov 3 ([Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/2026-midterms-prediction-market-odds-220150918.html)). By 6:55 PM ET, Kalshi's own live page had the House at 97.2%. At 8:06 PM it posted that Greg Abbott was "no longer favored" in Texas ([Kalshi News](https://news.kalshi.com/p/2026-midterm-election-night-live-projected-winners)). By about 9:10 PM, Mediaite reported Kalshi at 100% for the House ([Mediaite](https://www.mediaite.com/media/news/prediction-markets-say-dems-now-favored-to-win-the-house-by-an-absolutely-jaw-dropping-90seats/)). Your 8:44 PM screenshot (Senate Kalshi 98% and Polymarket 99%; House 99% on both) fits that path. It was taken before New York's polls closed, but after most Eastern polls had closed and real returns were arriving. So the markets were not 98% sure before anyone voted. They became 98% sure in the first hour or two of counting.

That does not make the observation trivial. The Senate jumped from about 75% to 98–99% roughly **three hours before AP's 11:40 PM call**. That is the market doing what it does well: turning scattered early returns into one number faster than a decision desk will commit. It is also exactly what partner networks chose not to show. CNN kept Kalshi odds off the Magic Wall and off election-night air, Fox relied on its own unit, and CBS kept odds out of newscasts ([Bloomberg via Tucson.com](https://tucson.com/news/nation-world/business/article_1500cb9f-37da-48fd-a340-e0f6706d498b.html)). Three caveats belong next to a 98¢ reading. First, Kalshi contracts settle only after designated media calls, so part of the remaining 1–2¢ is the cost of capital locked up until settlement, not doubt ([Covers](https://www.covers.com/politics/midterm-election-prediction-markets-payout-rules-2026)). Second, prices near the extremes are where the favorite-longshot bias and fees distort the most. Third, the realistic failure mode is not a random 2% surprise. It is everyone, markets included, misreading which votes are still uncounted. Your framing, "Bettors give X a 95% chance", with calls taken only from the AP data feed, matches what the critics recommend.

## Critics want disclosure and humility, not silence

The serious criticism focuses on presentation, not on banning the numbers. CJR's "A Bet Is Not a Poll" argues that traders are not a representative sample, that price moves can't be audited, and that readers "round" 70% up to certainty. It also notes that sports made up about 90% of Kalshi's 2025 volume ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/a-bet-is-not-a-poll-polymarket-kalshi-journalists-wary-predict-future.php)). The Intercept argues that odds crowd out coverage of substance ([The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2026/04/28/kalshi-polymarket-news-journalism-partnerships/)). Senator Chris Murphy called the CNN deal "wildly corrupt" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/journalism/polymarket-kalshi-cnn-cnbc-dow-jones/)). The New York Times told staff that betting on news events violates its principles ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/the-new-york-times-tells-staffers-that-betting-on-news-events-with-prediction-markets-is-a-violation-of-our-principles/)). A Reuters/Ipsos poll found 60% of US adults oppose election betting ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/kalshi-election-odds-cbs-news/)). Insider-trading cases, such as a new account turning $30k into more than $400k on a Maduro contract shortly before the U.S. operation, led to H.R. 7004 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/384200/rep-torres-targets-prediction-market-insider-trading-after-trader-nets-400k-on-maduros-capture-report)). The legal status of the markets is still contested across a circuit split ([Finance Magnates](https://financemagnates.com/fintech/federal-courts-now-disagree-on-kalshis-sports-contracts-new-jersey-wants-the-supreme-court-to-settle-the-fight)). Even the more sympathetic independent voices, such as Event Horizon's Dustin Gouker, say markets shouldn't be "a source of truth, or the only input" ([CJR interview](https://www.cjr.org/the-interview/are-prediction-markets-actually-good-for-journalism-kalshi-polymarket-dow-jones-cnn.php)).

## Conclusion

The market for "news about what the markets predict" has been captured from the supply side. The companies whose prices are the story pay for the newsletters, the TV tickers, the X wire accounts and even the election-night projection desk. That leaves a specific, defensible gap: an **independent, cross-exchange, plain-English, narrative product that puts odds in context**. It would show Kalshi next to Polymarket, explain why a price moved (which county reported, which vote batch is still out), show then-versus-now changes, and attach the caveats critics ask for, such as "traders, not a call" and "98¢ partly reflects settlement timing". The live broadcast form is the emptiest corner. Networks with partnerships keep odds off their election-night screens on purpose, Kalshi's feed is self-interested, Election Betting Odds is a table, and I found no open-source TV-style market display at all. Your election-night project already demonstrates that form.

The hard part is funding, not demand. Every outlet that grew either took exchange money or, like Adjacent, pivoted to infrastructure. Affiliate revenue is the obvious funding source and would undermine the independence that is the whole selling point. A credible independent version probably needs to be open source or a side project, reader-funded (Substack subscriptions, without sponsored embeds), or attached to an existing election-data product where odds are one layer next to official results. It would also need to commit to never earning per signup. Elections are only a few nights a year, but the same pipeline works for any current-affairs market. That year-round use is what would make it a product rather than a single night's broadcast.
